# Wiki: Alex Hormozi — $100M Offers

Compiled knowledge for EDGE OS. This is a working extract, not a substitute for the book.

Primary book: * $100M Offers: How To Make Offers So Good People Feel Stupid Saying No* — Alex Hormozi / Acquisition.com.

Operating translation lives in `systems/offers-money-models.md`.

## Core Thesis

Businesses grow in only three ways:
1. Get more customers
2. Get them to pay more
3. Get them to buy more times

The offer sits underneath all three. A weak offer makes traffic expensive. A Grand Slam Offer makes traffic cheaper because conversion, price, and word of mouth all improve.

Hormozi's line: make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no.

## Grand Slam Offer

A Grand Slam Offer is hard to compare to anything else in the market. It combines:
- Attractive promotion
- Unmatchable value proposition
- Premium price (or at least value-based price)
- Unbeatable guarantee
- A money model / payment terms that can get you paid to acquire customers

It solves the commodity problem: when buyers can only compare price, they pick the cheapest.

## Market First: The Starving Crowd

Offer strength matters less than whether the crowd is starving.

Hormozi's market filters:
- **Pain:** the pitch is the pain. Degree of pain sets price ceiling.
- **Purchasing power:** they can actually pay.
- **Easy to target:** you can find them with creative and media.
- **Growing:** do not build on a shrinking pond.

EDGE OS translation: this is Product + Distribution before Creative tricks. A Level 1 brand in a dead niche with a clever guarantee still dies.

He advises staying narrow until the niche can support serious scale, then broadening. Specificity raises price.

## Charge What It's Worth

Do not charge more than the product is worth.
Do charge much more than it costs to fulfill.

Premium price forces a better offer. Cheap price hides a weak offer and attracts worse customers.

For Indian D2C: premium is relative to category and Tier. A ₹1,499 hero can be premium in a ₹399 commodity aisle if the mechanism, proof, and unboxing justify it.

## The Value Equation

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood)
        / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Raise the top. Crush the bottom.

Important Hormozi notes:
- Perception is reality. The customer must *see* higher odds and lower effort.
- Fast beats free. A slower free thing often loses to a paid thing that works this week.
- Status and how they look in front of others often outruns logic.
- Give a fast first win so trust compounds.

## Building the Offer: Problems → Solutions → Trim and Stack

1. List every problem around getting the result (not just using the product).
2. Turn each problem into a named solution.
3. Brainstorm delivery vehicles as if the customer paid 10x (white glove) and 1/10th (one-to-many).
4. **Trim:** remove high-cost, low-perceived-value pieces.
5. **Stack:** keep high-value, low-incremental-cost pieces (guides, routines, WhatsApp support, minis, named kits).
6. Present the stack so the summed "value" dwarfs the price.

Sell the dream outcome, not the tool.

## Enhancing the Offer

### Scarcity (cut supply)
- Limited units / shades / festive batch
- Limited bonuses
- Never available in this form again
- Honest caps: only X orders/week because of handmade / cold-chain / founder QC

Fake scarcity destroys trust. Real scarcity raises desire.

### Urgency (cut time)
- Seasonal / festival windows that are real
- Cohort / drop dates
- Price or bonus expires
- "Price rises Monday"

### Bonuses
- Never lead with discounting the core if you can add bonuses
- Each bonus should kill a reason they would fail or hesitate
- Name the bonus. Show how it moves the value equation. Prove it. Paint the after-state.
- Bonus value should feel like it eclipses the core
- Tools, routines, checklists, minis, extended exchange, founder AMAs all work
- You can add bonuses after a yes (delight) or after a no (recovery)

### Guarantees (cut risk)
Hormozi types:
- **Unconditional:** strongest. Basically a paid trial.
- **Conditional:** they must do the required actions (use 14 days, send photo, finish sachets).
- **Anti-guarantee:** all sales final. Can raise status if demand is real.
- **Implied:** performance-based or "we only win if you win"

Formula: If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will Z.

For India: pair guarantees with prepaid incentives and RTO controls. Unconditional refund + unchecked COD is how brands die.

### Naming
Offers fatigue. Before changing the product, change:
1. Creative
2. Body copy
3. Headline / wrapper
4. Duration or enhancer

Hormozi-style name ingredients:
- Reason why (season, festival, restock)
- Avatar (who it's for)
- Goal (dream outcome)
- Interval (14-day, 90-day)
- Container (ritual, system, kit, protocol)

Alliteration and rhyme help memory. The name should make the offer incomparable to a single SKU on Amazon.

## Execution Notes for This Brain

- Offers are an EDGE multiplier. They do not replace product truth.
- Level 1: one named offer around one hero.
- Level 3+: separate prospecting offer and retention offer.
- Always ask: does this offer raise contribution and cash quality, or only CVR?
