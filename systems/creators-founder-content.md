# Creators and Founder Content

Creators and founder content are separate but connected. Creators help the brand borrow distribution, context, and social proof. Founder content helps the brand express taste, trust, and point of view.

## Founder Content

Founder content is a strategic asset when the founder can communicate:
- why the product exists
- what the category gets wrong
- what customers struggle with
- what the brand refuses to compromise on
- how the product works
- what proof exists
- what changed after customer feedback

Founder content should feel specific, not generic.

## Founder Content Formats

- founder selfie hook
- "why we made this"
- myth-busting
- product demo
- quality control behind the scenes
- packaging/order behind the scenes
- customer comment response
- founder reacts to bad category advice
- founder explains offer
- founder compares alternatives
- founder shares mistake/lesson
- founder tells brand origin

## Founder Content by Level

| Level | Role |
|---|---|
| 1 | Build trust, learn language, test angles fast |
| 2 | Turn winning founder angles into repeatable scripts |
| 3 | Use founder as recurring proof and category voice |
| 4 | Turn founder POV into brand memory and PR asset |
| 5 | Use founder selectively as one trust channel, not the only engine |

## Creator Content

Creator content is useful because it gives the brand:
- more faces
- more contexts
- more customer archetypes
- more usage occasions
- more raw footage
- more trust assets
- more insight into what different audiences respond to

## Creator Archetypes

- customer-like creator
- expert/educator
- lifestyle creator
- funny/skit creator
- reviewer/comparison creator
- transformation creator
- founder-like operator
- celebrity or macro personality
- niche community voice

## Creator Brief Template

Every creator brief should include:
- brand promise
- target customer
- awareness stage
- product edge
- one main pain/desire
- one proof point
- one demo requirement
- one objection to address
- required visual shots
- claims to avoid
- CTA or offer
- usage rights and editing rights

## Creator Seeding

Use seeding to discover:
- which creator archetypes understand the product
- which messages creators naturally repeat
- which product use cases appear
- which comments reveal buying objections
- which content can be repurposed into ads

Do not judge seeding only by immediate sales.

## Creator Whitelisting / Paid Amplification

Creator content becomes more powerful when the brand can use it in paid ads.

Before amplification, confirm:
- usage rights
- raw footage access
- allowed edits
- claims compliance
- landing page match
- creator identity fit
- performance metric

## Creator Scorecard

Track:
- content quality
- hook strength
- demo clarity
- brand fit
- comments quality
- paid performance
- CAC/CPA
- CVR
- RTO/order quality
- raw footage usefulness
- willingness to iterate

## Common Mistakes

- choosing creators only by follower count
- briefing creators with brand adjectives instead of customer problems
- not securing usage rights
- not turning creator content into paid variants
- using creators who create wrong expectations
- ignoring comments as customer research
- asking every creator to say the same generic script

## Practical Workflow

1. Identify 3-5 customer archetypes.
2. Match each archetype to 3-5 creator types.
3. Write briefs by awareness stage.
4. Seed or contract creators in batches.
5. Tag content by hook, persona, proof, demo, objection, and result.
6. Turn winning creator clips into ads, PDP proof, landing pages, email, and WhatsApp.
7. Rebook creators who create useful content, not only creators with vanity reach.
