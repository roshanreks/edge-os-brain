# Product Development and Launch System

Use this as the deep companion playbook. For the primary EDGE OS product operating system, load `systems/product-development.md` first, then return here for expanded launch, scorecard, and stage-gate detail.

Product development in EDGE OS is not random SKU expansion. It is the controlled process of turning customer insight into products, bundles, refills, accessories, formats, and experiences that increase conversion, LTV, retention, and brand defensibility.

## Core Principle

Do not launch products because the founder is bored.

Launch products when they:
- serve the same customer better
- increase LTV or repeat rate
- improve AOV or margin
- solve a known objection
- unlock a new awareness stage
- expand distribution
- deepen the brand's EDGE

## Product Development Paths

### 1. Hero Product Improvement

Make the current winner better.

Examples:
- better packaging
- improved formula/material
- better sizing
- more variants
- better instruction/use
- faster shipping
- stronger guarantee
- lower damage rate

Best for: Level 1-3.

### 2. Front-End Product

Makes first purchase easier.

Examples:
- starter kit
- trial pack
- mini
- lower-risk bundle
- sample kit
- entry accessory

Best for: reducing CAC and increasing first purchase conversion.

### 3. Back-End Product

Increases LTV after the first purchase.

Examples:
- refill
- second-step routine
- premium version
- accessory
- care kit
- subscription
- membership
- replenishment pack

Best for: Level 3-5 when acquisition is working but LTV needs help.

### 4. Adjacent Product

Solves the next problem for the same customer.

Examples:
- skincare cleanser -> moisturizer
- hair oil -> shampoo
- cookware pan -> utensils
- protein snack -> drink mix

Best for: brands with clear customer profile and repeat demand.

### 5. Category Expansion

Moves into a new category or buyer use case.

Best for: Level 4-5 only, unless demand is unusually obvious.

Risk:
- dilutes edge
- stresses inventory/cash
- requires new education
- can confuse creative and store architecture

## Product Development Stage Gates

### Gate 1: Customer Truth

Evidence:
- reviews
- support tickets
- returns/refunds
- competitor complaints
- community comments
- post-purchase survey
- search demand
- creator/customer requests

Question: Do customers already show this need?

### Gate 2: Business Fit

Evidence:
- margin estimate
- shipping cost
- packaging cost
- RTO risk
- supplier MOQ
- lead time
- inventory cash requirement
- expected AOV/LTV effect

Question: Can this product make economic sense?

### Gate 3: Brand Fit

Evidence:
- fits positioning
- fits customer profile
- strengthens EDGE
- does not confuse category memory

Question: Does this make the brand easier or harder to understand?

### Gate 4: Demand Signal

Evidence:
- static ad test
- waitlist
- preorder
- landing page test
- creator seeding
- survey
- small batch sell-through

Question: Will people act, not just say it is a good idea?

### Gate 5: Launch Readiness

Evidence:
- PDP/landing page
- creative angles
- offer architecture
- inventory
- fulfillment SOP
- support scripts
- review/social proof plan
- launch calendar

Question: Can we launch without damaging trust or cash flow?

### Gate 6: Post-Launch Truth

Evidence:
- sell-through
- CAC
- CVR
- AOV
- contribution margin
- review quality
- support issues
- returns/RTO
- second purchase

Question: Should we scale, iterate, bundle, or kill?

## Product Testing Methods

- founder/customer survey
- competitor review mining
- Amazon/marketplace review mining
- static ad concept test
- "coming soon" waitlist
- preorder/drop
- limited batch
- creator seeding
- sample kit
- retail/offline sampling
- WhatsApp poll for existing buyers
- post-purchase "what should we make next?"

## Product Launch Playbook

### Pre-Launch

- define product role: front-end, back-end, adjacent, expansion
- define target customer and awareness stage
- write one-line promise
- create proof plan
- create waitlist or teaser if relevant
- prepare creator/customer content
- prepare landing page/PDP
- define offer
- define inventory and sell-through target
- define launch channels
- define post-mortem date before launch

### Launch Week

- send to existing customers if relevant
- use founder story and product reason
- deploy paid creative by awareness stage
- show social proof as quickly as possible
- watch inventory, support, shipping, and RTO daily
- capture comments and objections
- update PDP/FAQ quickly

### Post-Launch

- compare actuals vs forecast
- identify top creative angles
- identify top objections
- review channel and cohort quality
- calculate contribution margin
- read support tickets and reviews
- decide scale, iterate, bundle, or kill

## Product Development by Level

| Level | Product job |
|---|---|
| 1 | prove hero product demand and repeat signal |
| 2 | improve hero, avoid random SKU sprawl, test simple bundles |
| 3 | build front-end/back-end product logic and controlled adjacent tests |
| 4 | create product roadmap, launch SOP, portfolio economics |
| 5 | manage product portfolio, category expansion, channel-specific assortment |

## Product Quality Score (100 Points)

- Customer pain/desire evidence: 15
- Differentiation/EDGE: 15
- Repeat/LTV potential: 15
- Gross margin and contribution potential: 15
- Creative demonstrability: 10
- Store/PDP explainability: 10
- Fulfillment/RTO feasibility: 10
- Brand fit: 10

Interpretation:
- 85-100: strong candidate for scale
- 70-84: test with stage gates
- 50-69: needs clearer demand or economics
- below 50: do not fund beyond research

## Launch Score (100 Points)

- Clear product role: 10
- Forecast and inventory plan: 10
- Offer architecture: 10
- PDP/landing page readiness: 15
- Creative angles ready: 15
- Proof/social proof plan: 10
- Existing customer/retention plan: 10
- Fulfillment/support readiness: 10
- Post-mortem metrics defined: 10

## Common Mistakes

- launching too many SKUs before the hero is stable
- using product launches to distract from weak creative
- ignoring supplier MOQ and cash cycle
- launching without proof plan
- treating a launch like one campaign instead of a full GTM system
- judging launch success only by revenue
- keeping products alive because the team likes them
- not feeding support/review learnings back into product and copy
