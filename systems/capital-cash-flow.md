# Capital Allocation and Cash Flow

E-commerce brands can grow revenue and still run out of cash. EDGE OS treats capital allocation as a growth system, not finance admin.

## Core Principle

Spend should follow evidence. Cash should be protected from unproven inventory, weak creative, high RTO, slow-moving SKUs, and vanity brand activity.

## Cash Conversion Cycle

The simple formula:

`Cash Conversion Cycle = Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding - Days Payables Outstanding`

For D2C, also consider:
- prepaid vs COD mix
- payment gateway settlement
- courier remittance timing
- RTO delay
- supplier advances
- inventory lead time
- ad spend paid before revenue is collected
- discounting and refunds

## Contribution Margin View

Track contribution after:
- product cost
- packaging
- shipping
- payment gateway/COD charges
- RTO and returns
- discounts
- ad spend
- creator/affiliate commissions where relevant

ROAS without contribution margin can mislead the founder.

## Capital Allocation Buckets

1. Proven hero inventory
2. Creative testing and production
3. Paid media scaling
4. Store/CRO/speed improvements
5. Retention and CRM
6. Fulfillment/RTO reduction
7. Creator/association experiments
8. New product testing
9. Team and tools
10. Brand bets

## Level-Specific Allocation

| Level | Allocation priority |
|---|---|
| 1 | product validation, small creative tests, basic store, initial inventory |
| 2 | proven inventory, creative volume, retention basics, CRO fixes |
| 3 | creative system, hero inventory, landing pages, fulfillment controls, retention |
| 4 | team owners, creative ops, retention, inventory forecasting, association tests |
| 5 | portfolio allocation, leadership, data, brand memory, channel expansion, product bets |

## Product Testing Budget

Use stage gates instead of emotional launches.

Directional guardrails:
- Level 1: small batches and ad tests, usually Rs 10k-50k per concept/SKU test if economics allow
- Level 2: Rs 50k-1.5L for stronger product/offer validation
- Level 3: Rs 1-3L or 5-8% of monthly revenue across controlled tests
- Level 4: Rs 3-7L or 5-10% of monthly revenue across staged tests
- Level 5: portfolio-level test budget with clear gates, often split by product line or innovation theme

Pass/fail signals:
- CTR/interest
- add-to-cart rate
- purchase CVR
- CAC/CPA
- contribution margin
- qualitative feedback
- repeat or usage signal
- support burden
- refund/RTO risk

## Media Budget Testing

Directional guidelines:
- Level 1: 20-30% of media budget can be testing
- Level 2: 15-25%
- Level 3: 12-20%
- Level 4: 10-15%
- Level 5: 8-15%, plus separate innovation budgets

Do not eliminate testing during scale. That only creates future creative fatigue.

## Inventory Discipline

Before buying more inventory:
- Is demand proven or assumed?
- What is current sell-through?
- What is reorder lead time?
- What is gross margin?
- What is RTO/return risk?
- Is cash needed for ads before revenue comes back?
- What happens if the creative winner dies?
- Can the SKU be bundled or liquidated if slow?

## Discount Discipline

Discounts can help conversion but can damage:
- margin
- brand perception
- customer quality
- repeat behavior
- future willingness to pay

Test:
- bundles
- free shipping thresholds
- prepaid incentives
- gifts
- loyalty points
- limited drops
- subscriptions/refills

Do not use discounting as the only growth lever.

## 13-Week Cash Forecast

At Level 3+, maintain a rolling 13-week view:
- opening cash
- expected sales
- ad spend
- inventory payments
- payroll/freelancers
- agency/tool costs
- shipping/COD settlements
- refunds/RTO
- tax/statutory obligations
- closing cash

## Cash Review Checklist

- Which SKU truly generates cash?
- Which SKU traps cash?
- What is contribution margin by channel?
- What is CAC payback timing?
- How much cash is stuck in COD/RTO cycles?
- What inventory will run out?
- What inventory is dead or slow?
- Which experiments deserve more budget?
- Which expenses are vanity?
