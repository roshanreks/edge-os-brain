## Product Truth

Repeat purchase is the truth serum.

If 90-day repeat is weak in a category that should repeat, you do not have a media problem. You have a product or expectation problem.

Hormozi's starving-crowd filter applies before SKU brainstorms:
- Real pain
- Ability to pay
- Easy to target
- Growing pond

Schwartz applies to product claims:
- Crowded categories need a **unique mechanism**, not "premium quality"
- Mechanism must be demonstrable on camera and on the PDP

## The Hero Rule

Every level has one hero (or one hero system).

The hero is the product that:
- Sells with less convincing
- Produces better comments
- Has better repeat or higher contribution
- Can carry kits, upsells, and continuity

Do not launch SKU #7 because the founder is bored of SKU #1.

## Product Jobs

A product must do at least one of these better than alternatives:
- Result
- Feel / sensory
- Convenience
- Trust / safety
- Identity / status
- Value / completeness (the kit)

If the team cannot say this in one sentence a creator can repeat, the product is not ready for scale.

## Development Pipeline

### 1. Mine
- Own reviews and 1-star themes
- Competitor Amazon/Flipkart/Nykaa reviews
- WhatsApp tickets
- Returns reasons
- Winning ad comments
- Founder customer calls (L1: 20–50 conversations)

### 2. Name the next problem
Hormozi: the first purchase creates the next problem. That is your upsell SKU or bonus, not a random new category.

Examples:
- Serum creates "what do I moisturise with"
- Snack creates "what do I eat at 5pm at the office"
- Cleaner creates "what do I use on glass vs floor"

### 3. Design the vehicle
- Trial / sachet / mini (attraction, downsell)
- Hero single (core)
- Routine kit (anchor / buy X get Y)
- Refill / subscribe (continuity)
- Premium / limited (association, gifting)

### 4. Claim and proof
Every new claim needs a proof type before launch:
- Demo
- Ingredient / material
- Customer
- Expert (compliant)
- Process / origin

No proof = no scale.

### 5. Stage gates

| Gate | Pass rule |
|---|---|
| Concept | Pain + buyer + mechanism in one page |
| Offer | Value equation score not embarrassing |
| Creative | 3–5 concepts can be shot cheaply |
| Demand | Spend gate hit or waitlist quality |
| Fulfillment | 50–100 orders without support explosion |
| Repeat | Early reorder / usage signal if category should repeat |
| Scale | Inventory and cash plan exist |

Kill at any gate. Sunk cost is not a strategy.

## Level Rules

### Level 1
- One hero. Maybe one trial size.
- Improve the product from customer language.
- Packaging must be reliable, not Instagram-first.
- Do not build a 12-SKU catalogue.

### Level 2
- Protect the hero.
- Add the *next-problem* complement if contribution allows.
- Build the first kit.
- Start collecting structured feedback (post-purchase WhatsApp).

### Level 3
- Adjacent SKUs only if they multiply the edge.
- Product testing cap usually 5–8% of monthly revenue.
- Each new SKU must have a money-model job (attract / upsell / continuity).
- Formula / fit / flavour iterations beat category hopping.

### Level 4
- Portfolio on purpose: hero, attractor, expander, premium, continuity.
- Launch calendar tied to festivals and inventory, not founder mood.
- Packaging can become a content and retention asset if economics work.

### Level 5
- Customer data launches products.
- New lines get the same stage gates as a startup.
- Celebrity SKUs still need product truth.
- Prune. Killing SKUs is product development.

## India-Specific Product Constraints

- **Climate and context:** humidity, hard water, spice oil, shared bathrooms, small kitchens, two-wheeler delivery. Global formulas fail here for boring reasons.
- **Trust:** fakes and me-too listings. Pack, seal, batch, and "how to spot original" matter.
- **Regulation:** beauty/wellness claims. Do not write Hormozi dream outcomes that FSSAI/ADS rules will punish.
- **Q-comm / marketplace:** different pack sizes and margins. Design a D2C kit and a marketplace single separately.
- **RTO:** products that surprise people (size, smell, colour, quantity) create returns. Description is part of the product.

## Product Brief (one page)

1. Who is it for?
2. What pain / desire?
3. Why this mechanism?
4. What is the first win and when?
5. What do they use instead today?
6. What will they say in a UGC video?
7. What is the offer vehicle (trial / hero / kit / refill)?
8. What is the next product this creates?
9. Unit economics after RTO and gift?
10. What would make us kill it?

## Anti-Patterns

- Launching because a competitor launched
- Building for the founder's taste circle in Bandra
- Changing formula every month so no review base can form
- Kits that are leftovers, not a system
- Premium price with commodity product
- "New flavour" instead of fixing the hero
