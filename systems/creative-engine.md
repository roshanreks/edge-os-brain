# Creative Engine System

Creative is one of the five EDGE components and becomes the primary growth lever as spend increases.

## Testing Volume Guidelines (from original map + 2026 data)

| Level | Monthly Revenue | New Concepts / Month | Notes |
|-------|-----------------|----------------------|-------|
| 1 | 0–5 Lacs | 8–20 | Low-effort dominant |
| 2 | 5–20 Lacs | 20–50 | Start systematic batches |
| 3 | 20–50 Lacs | 50–120 | Volume becomes critical |
| 4 | 50L–1 Cr | 100–250+ | Dedicated creative ops needed |
| 5 | 1 Cr+ | 200–500+ | Industrial creative production |

## Effort Levels

**Low-effort**  
Static carousels, simple UGC, phone talking-head, text overlays, customer screenshots, screen recordings. Fast, cheap, short lifespan. Essential at every level as the testing base.

**High-effort**  
Studio video, cinematic UGC, scripted founder stories, polished motion, multi-scene pieces, celebrity/influencer content. Higher win rate and longer lifespan when they hit. Introduce progressively from Level 2 onward.

**Founder content**  
Never stops. Highest authenticity leverage. At higher levels it becomes brand equity.

## Awareness Mapping for Creative
- Levels 1–2: Heavy Product Aware + Most Aware
- Level 3+: Must expand into Solution Aware and Problem Aware to keep growing the audience pool
- Level 5: Deliberate Unaware content for top-of-funnel brand building

## Operating Rules
- 10–20% of media budget should stay in pure testing
- Winners get graduated into scaling campaigns
- Creative fatigue is real — refresh cadence matters more than perfection at scale
- Volume of good concepts beats a small number of “perfect” ads
- Distinguish new concepts from minor variants. A new headline on the same visual is not the same as a new customer, problem, mechanism, proof type, or format.
- Use statics as cheap angle tests, then turn winners into UGC scripts, founder videos, PDP sections, landing page heroes, and creator briefs.
- Creative quality must be judged with order quality: CAC, CVR, AOV, contribution, RTO, support load, and repeat behavior.

## Outlier Creative Reality

Most ads will fail. The system exists to find and scale outliers, not to make every ad a winner.

Practical implications:
- Test enough concepts to make outlier discovery possible.
- Protect testing budget even when scaling winners.
- Kill based on spend/signal thresholds, not personal taste.
- Allow a small share of tests to run longer when they are new categories, seasonal concepts, or education-heavy ideas.
- Build a searchable creative library so old learning compounds.

## Creative Concept vs Variant

### Concept

A concept changes the reason a customer might care.

Examples:
- new pain
- new persona
- new mechanism
- new proof type
- new product use case
- new awareness stage
- new format

### Variant

A variant changes execution around the same idea.

Examples:
- new first frame
- new headline
- new hook wording
- new edit pace
- new background
- new CTA

Both matter, but only concepts expand the brand's demand surface.

## Production System (as you scale)
- Weekly or bi-weekly creative batches
- Clear naming conventions and tracking
- Separate testing vs scaling campaigns
- Feedback loop from performance data back into new concepts

## Outlier Math (CTC 2026, translated)

Creative performance is a power law, not a bell curve.

Common Thread Collective, across 170+ ecommerce brands:
- The top ~**3.5%** of ads generate ~**66%** of spend
- ~**79%** of ads never reach meaningful spend before they are killed
- You cannot reliably pick the outlier in the edit bay
- If you need one new outlier a month, you must test on the order of ~25–30 distinct ads, not 5 pretty ones
- ~70% of mature volume should be variants of proven frameworks; ~30% net-new concepts

This is why Level 3+ volume looks "insane" to Level 1 founders. It is demand planning, not art school.

## Creative Scorecard

Score each ad after the spend gate. Do not marry ROAS.

| Score | Ask |
|---|---|
| Hook | Did the first 1–2 seconds earn the right person? |
| Clarity | Did they understand the product before they left? |
| Desire | Did the dream outcome show up? |
| Proof | Was there a reason to believe? |
| Offer | Was the next action obvious? |
| Match | Did the landing page continue the same sentence? |
| Economics | CPA vs break-even, and order quality (RTO, prepaid, complaints) |
| Fatigue | Is frequency eating it? |

Leading indicators (directional, not religion):
- Thumbstop / 3-sec: weak under ~15%, healthier 20–30%+
- Outbound CTR: weak under ~0.6%, workable 0.8–1.5%, strong 1.5–3%+
- Clicks without ATC → page/offer problem, not "need more budget"
- ATC without purchase → checkout, trust, COD, shipping

## Offer Creative Rule (Hormozi)

Advertise the **Grand Slam stack**, not only the object.

Bad: pretty bottle, logo, "shop now"
Better: problem → mechanism → proof → named kit → guarantee → CTA

Attraction ads and continuity ads are different products. Do not run a subscribe-and-save script to cold Problem-Aware traffic.
