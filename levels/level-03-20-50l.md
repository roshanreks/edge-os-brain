# Level 3: 20-50 Lacs Monthly

**Primary Job:** Turn early traction into a repeatable acquisition machine while expanding beyond the warmest audiences.

Level 3 is where many brands hit the first real wall. The product works, a few creatives work, and the store is converting enough to spend. But the founder now starts feeling CAC pressure, creative fatigue, operational leaks, and cash stress at the same time.

The job is not to "try random new things." The job is to extract the proven edge, build systems around it, and deliberately move into broader awareness stages.

## What This Level Feels Like
- Revenue is real but still volatile week to week
- One or two winning angles still drive a large share of sales
- Meta performance is sensitive to creative freshness
- Founder is still involved in creative approvals, offers, and firefighting
- Inventory, cash, COD/RTO, and fulfillment delays start creating growth ceilings
- Team or freelancers exist, but systems are not fully documented
- The brand has proof, but not yet enough memory or depth

## Key Metrics (Healthy Ranges)
- Monthly revenue: 20-50 Lacs
- Ad spend as % of revenue: 20-28%
- Media testing allocation: 12-20% of media budget
- Creative testing volume: 50-120 new concepts per month
- Store conversion rate: generally aim for 1.8-2.8%+ depending on category, AOV, and traffic mix
- 90-day repeat purchase rate: 25-35%+ is strong for repeatable categories
- Product testing cap: usually 5-8% of monthly revenue for new SKU validation unless pre-orders, waitlists, or existing demand reduce risk
- Contribution margin: track contribution after product cost, shipping, gateway/COD costs, RTO, discounts, and ad spend
- Cash conversion cycle: visible and actively managed, not guessed

## EDGE Focus at This Level
1. **Creative** - Build velocity and awareness range. The creative engine becomes the main growth system.
2. **Distribution** - Move beyond one paid channel or one audience pool.
3. **Product** - Protect the hero product and test adjacent offers carefully.
4. **Branding** - Sharpen category positioning so the brand becomes easier to remember.
5. **Association** - Start using creators and selective influencers as proof and distribution.

**Core rule:** If Level 2 was "make more of what works," Level 3 is "make what works scalable, measurable, and harder to copy."

## Awareness Priorities
At this level, Product Aware and Most Aware traffic begins to saturate.

Creative must deliberately expand into:
- **Solution Aware:** "You know the type of solution, but here is why our mechanism is different."
- **Problem Aware:** "You feel this pain, but you have not connected it to this product yet."
- **Unaware light:** founder stories, identity content, routines, category education, and cultural hooks.

## Creative & Content Recommendations
- Keep low-effort testing as the base: UGC, demos, founder talking heads, customer screenshots, simple statics, comparison frames
- Turn winners into high-effort versions: better hooks, stronger scripts, cleaner edits, multi-scene shoots, category explainers
- Build 6-10 angle families, not just isolated ads
- Start a weekly creative review: hook, hold rate, CTR, CVR, CAC, thumbstop, comment quality, and post-purchase survey signal
- Use creators for angle discovery, not only polished endorsements
- Introduce advertorials, quiz funnels, comparison pages, and problem education pages for colder traffic

## Ad Spend & Testing
- Spend should scale only when contribution margin and fulfillment can handle volume
- Testing budget should be protected even when winners are scaling
- New product tests should have a clear pass/fail rule before launch:
  - minimum traffic or spend threshold
  - target CAC or CPA range
  - target add-to-cart and purchase signal
  - customer feedback quality
  - repeat or usage signal if relevant
- Do not let product testing steal inventory or budget from the proven hero unless the hero is weakening

## Conversion / Store Priorities
- PDP must mirror the strongest ad angles
- Above the fold needs: clear promise, product visual, proof, price/offer, CTA, delivery/payment trust
- Build landing pages for cold education when PDP is too direct
- Improve mobile speed, image weight, checkout friction, COD clarity, and shipping expectations
- Add stronger proof: reviews, before/after, creator quotes, expert proof, press, guarantee, demo GIF/video
- Test bundles and AOV lifts carefully; do not crush CVR just to raise AOV

## Fulfillment & Operations
- RTO is now a growth lever, not an ops detail
- Build COD verification, address checks, WhatsApp confirmation, NDR follow-up, and clear delivery expectation systems
- Track cancellation reasons and repeat customer complaints weekly
- Packaging should be reliable, damage-resistant, and brand-consistent
- Document SOPs for order flow, returns, exchange, support, and creator seeding

## Capital Allocation Priority
1. Hero product inventory and replenishment reliability
2. Creative production and testing system
3. Store/CRO fixes that improve blended economics
4. Retention flows and post-purchase education
5. Distribution experiments with clear payback rules
6. New SKU tests only after the hero engine is protected

## Offer & Money Model (Hormozi @ L3)
Now you need a real sequence: Attraction → Upsell → Downsell → Continuity.

Install:
- Cold-traffic attraction offer (trial, decoy table, named ritual) different from retargeting offer
- Post-purchase stack: one complement, then one downsell
- Subscribe & save or refill on consumables (continuity bonus, not a buried checkbox)
- Win-your-money-back only if CX can honour it and COD is controlled
- Weekly offer scorecard (take rates + contribution + payback)

Marketplace/q-comm may only get a simpler offer. Keep the full model on owned D2C.

Deep system: `systems/offers-money-models.md`

## Testing @ L3
- 50–120 concepts. 6–10 angle families.
- Spend gate: roughly ₹4,000–10,000 per concept
- Creative scorecard: hook, CTR, ATC, CPA, comments, RTO by ad
- CRO: advertorial/quiz vs PDP for Problem/Solution-aware traffic
- Proper A/B only on high-traffic surfaces; still no tiny tests on a slow site
- CTC math applies: most ads die; volume is how you find the 3.5%

Deep system: `systems/testing-and-experimentation.md`

## Product Development @ L3
- New SKUs must have a money-model job
- Cap product experiments (~5–8% of revenue)
- Iterate the hero (formula, fit, flavour, pack) before category hopping

Deep system: `systems/product-development.md`

## How To Scale @ L3
Scale the engine: creative throughput, cold pages, RTO system, money model.

Do not scale spend faster than those four.

Deep system: `systems/scaling.md`

## Common Traps
- Treating Level 3 like Level 1 and testing everything randomly
- Scaling ad spend without enough creative volume
- Building a "pretty brand" that does not improve conversion, recall, or trust
- Overusing discounts to force revenue
- Letting RTO and delayed COD remittance hide true profitability
- Hiring before processes are clear
- Launching too many SKUs without understanding the original edge
- Copying US continuity dark patterns onto WhatsApp-first buyers

## 90-Day Action Checklist
- [ ] Document the top 5 winning angles and map them to awareness stages
- [ ] Produce 50-120 new concepts/month across 6-10 angle families
- [ ] Build a creative scorecard that connects ad metrics to store and order quality
- [ ] Create one cold-traffic landing page or advertorial for the strongest problem-aware angle
- [ ] Improve the PDP above the fold and proof section
- [ ] Set up RTO, cancellation, and NDR dashboards
- [ ] Create retention flows for first-to-second purchase and replenishment
- [ ] Define a product testing budget and pass/fail rule
- [ ] Document weekly founder/team operating rhythm

**Exit criteria to Level 4:** Revenue holds in the upper half of the band, creative production is no longer founder-memory dependent, CAC is controlled through angle expansion, fulfillment can handle higher volume, and contribution margin is visible enough to allocate capital confidently.
